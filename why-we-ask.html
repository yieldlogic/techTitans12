WHY WE ASK




Every Question Has a Purpose

Our Yield Calculator asks for specific details about your farm — not to make things complicated, but because each answer changes your results. Here's exactly why we ask, and how each piece of information helps you get a more accurate estimate.

Why We Ask: Where Is Your Farm Located?

"Two farms 50 km apart can have completely different seasons."

What location tells us:

  • Climate zone — Average temperature, frost dates, and growing season length
  • Rainfall patterns — How much rain you typically get, and when
  • Soil region — What types of soil are common in your area
  • Local pests & diseases — Some regions have specific pest pressures

How it affects your results:

A maize farm in Mpumalanga (high rainfall, warm) will produce very differently from one in the Free State (drier, cooler). By knowing your location, we adjust your expected yield for your actual climate — not a national average.

Example: A farmer in a high-rainfall area may not need irrigation. A farmer in a dry region almost certainly does. Same crop, different advice.

Why We Ask: What Is Your Soil Type?

"Soil is the foundation — everything grows from it."

What soil type tells us:

  • Water retention — How well your soil holds moisture between rains
  • Nutrient availability — How easily roots can access food
  • Drainage — Whether water logs or flows away
  • Root growth — Some soils are easy for roots to spread in; others are not

Quick guide to soil types:

Loamy

The ideal mix of sand, silt, and clay. Great drainage, holds nutrients. Best yield potential.

Clay Loam / Silty

Very good for most crops. Holds water and nutrients well. May drain slowly.

Clay

Holds water and nutrients, but can become waterlogged and hard to work.

Sandy Loam / Sandy

Drains fast, loses water and nutrients quickly. Needs more irrigation.

Peaty

Acidic and variable. Great for some crops, poor for others.

Example: The same maize seed planted in loamy soil can yield up to 35% more than in sandy soil — with the same water and fertilizer.

Why We Ask: Are You Using Irrigation?

"Water is the single biggest factor in yield — after soil."

What irrigation tells us:

  • Water security — Can you keep crops hydrated during dry spells?
  • Consistency — Irrigated farms have more predictable yields than rain-fed ones
  • Efficiency — Different irrigation types deliver water very differently
  • Cost — Irrigation costs money, so we factor that into your profit

Irrigation types and their impact:

Type Efficiency Yield Boost
Drip 💧 Best — water goes straight to roots +25%
Sprinkler / Pivot Good — covers evenly +18%
Flood / Furrow Fair — lots of evaporation +10%
Rain-fed only Depends on weather Baseline
Example: Two maize farmers with identical soil and rainfall. The one with drip irrigation can expect 25% more yield — because water goes where roots actually need it, not everywhere else.

Why We Ask: Have You Noticed Any Pests?

"Pests don't just damage crops — they damage your income."

What pest information tells us:

  • Stress on the crop — Even mild pest pressure reduces yield
  • Urgency — Severe pests need immediate action, not "wait and see"
  • Hidden losses — Some pests reduce quality, not just quantity
  • Cost impact — Pesticide treatment adds cost; ignoring pests costs yield

How we read your answer:

What You Report Yield Impact What It Means
None / Nothing 0% loss Ideal — keep monitoring weekly
Mild / Few / Minor −8% loss Treat early — still manageable
Moderate / Some −18% loss Act now — every week counts
Severe / Infestation −30% loss Immediate action required
Example: An untreated aphid infestation on wheat can reduce yield by up to 30%. Spotting it early and treating it costs far less than losing a third of your harvest.

What Is a Hectare?

"A hectare is just a way of measuring land — like a kilogram measures weight."

The simple definition:

A hectare (ha) is a unit of land area equal to 10,000 square metres — that's a square that's 100 metres × 100 metres.

100 m 1 HECTARE 100 m

Rough comparisons (useful if you think in other units):

  • 1 hectare ≈ 2.47 acres
  • 1 hectare ≈ 1.4 soccer fields
  • 1 hectare ≈ 100 parking spaces
  • 100 hectares ≈ 1 square kilometre

Why it matters for your estimate:

Yield is measured per hectare. So if you know your field is 5 hectares, and your expected yield is 7,000 kg/ha, then your total harvest is:

5 ha × 7,000 kg/ha = 35,000 kg (35 tons)

How to measure your land if you don't know:

  • Use a GPS app on your phone (many are free)
  • Check your title deed or land survey documents
  • Ask your local agricultural extension officer
  • Rough estimate: pace out the length and width, multiply them, then divide by 10,000
Tip: If you're not sure, it's better to give us an approximate number than to skip the field. You can always update it later as your records improve.
